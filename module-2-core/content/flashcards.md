# Electronics Flashcards

## Card 1

**Q:** What is the definition of electrical voltage?

**A:** The electrical pressure or potential difference that drives charge through a circuit.

---

## Card 2

**Q:** What is the standard unit of measurement for voltage?

**A:** The Volt ($V$).

---

## Card 3

**Q:** In electrical formulas, which symbol is used to represent current?

**A:** $I$

---

## Card 4

**Q:** How is electrical current defined?

**A:** The rate of flow of electrical charge through a conductor.

---

## Card 5

**Q:** What is the standard unit of measurement for electrical current?

**A:** The Ampere or Amp ($A$).

---

## Card 6

**Q:** What does 'resistance' measure in an electrical circuit?

**A:** The opposition to the flow of electrical current.

---

## Card 7

**Q:** What is the standard unit of measurement for resistance?

**A:** The Ohm ($\Omega$).

---

## Card 8

**Q:** Which Greek letter is used as the symbol for Ohms?

**A:** $\Omega$

---

## Card 9

**Q:** What is the primary formula for Ohm's Law?

**A:** $V = I \times R$

---

## Card 10

**Q:** According to Ohm's Law, how do you calculate current ($I$) if voltage and resistance are known?

**A:** $I = \frac{V}{R}$

---

## Card 11

**Q:** According to Ohm's Law, how do you calculate resistance ($R$) if voltage and current are known?

**A:** $R = \frac{V}{I}$

---

## Card 12

**Q:** If the voltage in a circuit is increased while resistance remains constant, what happens to the current?

**A:** The current increases.

---

## Card 13

**Q:** If the resistance in a circuit is increased while voltage remains constant, what happens to the current?

**A:** The current decreases.

---

## Card 14

**Q:** What is the definition of electrical power?

**A:** The rate at which electrical energy is transferred or consumed by a circuit.

---

## Card 15

**Q:** What is the standard unit of measurement for electrical power?

**A:** The Watt ($W$).

---

## Card 16

**Q:** What is the formula used to calculate electrical power ($P$)?

**A:** $P = V \times I$

---

## Card 17

**Q:** Term: Direct Current (DC)

**A:** Definition: Electrical current that flows in only one constant direction. Example: Power from a battery.

---

## Card 18

**Q:** Term: Alternating Current (AC)

**A:** Definition: Electrical current that periodically reverses its direction. Example: Power from a wall outlet.

---

## Card 19

**Q:** Why is Direct Current (DC) typically used for Arduino projects?

**A:** Electronic logic and microcontrollers require a steady, constant voltage to function.

---

## Card 20

**Q:** Which type of current is supplied by standard household wall outlets?

**A:** Alternating Current (AC).

---

## Card 21

**Q:** How many paths for current flow exist in a series circuit?

**A:** Exactly one path.

---

## Card 22

**Q:** In a series circuit, how does the current ($I$) behave across different components?

**A:** The current remains the same at all points in the circuit.

---

## Card 23

**Q:** In a series circuit, how is the total voltage ($V_{total}$) related to the individual component voltages?

**A:** The total voltage is the sum of the voltages across each component.

---

## Card 24

**Q:** What is the formula for calculating total resistance ($R_{total}$) in a series circuit?

**A:** $R_{total} = R_1 + R_2 + ... + R_n$

---

## Card 25

**Q:** If one component in a series circuit fails or is disconnected, what happens to the rest of the circuit?

**A:** The circuit is broken and all current flow stops.

---

## Card 26

**Q:** How are components arranged in a parallel circuit?

**A:** Components are connected across the same two nodes, creating multiple paths for current.

---

## Card 27

**Q:** In a parallel circuit, how does the voltage ($V$) behave across different branches?

**A:** The voltage is the same across every parallel branch.

---

## Card 28

**Q:** In a parallel circuit, how is the total current ($I_{total}$) related to the currents in individual branches?

**A:** The total current is the sum of the currents flowing through each branch.

---

## Card 29

**Q:** If you add more resistors in parallel, what happens to the total resistance of the circuit?

**A:** The total resistance decreases.

---

## Card 30

**Q:** In a parallel circuit, the total resistance is always _____ than the resistance of the smallest individual resistor.

**A:** lower

---

## Card 31

**Q:** If one branch of a parallel circuit is disconnected, what happens to the current in the other branches?

**A:** The other branches continue to receive power and function normally.

---

## Card 32

**Q:** Formula: $V = I \times R$. What does the variable $R$ represent?

**A:** Resistance.

---

## Card 33

**Q:** A circuit has a voltage of $10V$ and a resistance of $5\Omega$. What is the current?

**A:** $2A$

---

## Card 34

**Q:** A device draws $2A$ of current at $12V$. How much power ($P$) does it consume?

**A:** $24W$

---

## Card 35

**Q:** The _____ is the unit used to describe the rate of energy transfer over time.

**A:** Watt

---

## Card 36

**Q:** If two $10\Omega$ resistors are connected in series, what is the total resistance?

**A:** $20\Omega$

---

## Card 37

**Q:** Why does a parallel circuit allow multiple devices to operate independently?

**A:** Because each branch provides a separate, complete path for the current to flow.

---

## Card 38

**Q:** In the context of Ohm's Law, current and resistance have an _____ relationship.

**A:** inverse

---

## Card 39

**Q:** Concept: Potential Difference

**A:** Definition: Another term for voltage, describing the difference in electrical charge between two points.

---

## Card 40

**Q:** Which circuit configuration is typically used for house wiring so that turning off one light doesn't kill the power to others?

**A:** Parallel circuit.

---

## Card 41

**Q:** What physical quantity is calculated by multiplying $V$ by $I$?

**A:** Electrical Power ($P$).

---

## Card 42

**Q:** In a circuit with a $9V$ battery and a $100\Omega$ resistor, what formula would you use to find the flow of charge?

**A:** $I = \frac{V}{R}$

---

## Card 43

**Q:** True or False: In a series circuit, adding more resistors reduces the total current.

**A:** True (because total resistance increases).

---

## Card 44

**Q:** Formula: $P = V \times I$. If you double the voltage and the current stays the same, what happens to the power?

**A:** The power doubles.

---

## Card 45

**Q:** What happens to the total resistance in a series circuit if you remove one of three resistors and replace it with a wire?

**A:** The total resistance decreases.

---

## Card 46

**Q:** If three identical lamps are in parallel across a $5V$ source, what is the voltage across the second lamp?

**A:** $5V$

---

## Card 47

**Q:** Cloze: In _____ current, the flow of electrons behaves like a tide, moving back and forth.

**A:** alternating

---

## Card 48

**Q:** Cloze: In _____ current, the flow of electrons behaves like a steady stream in one direction.

**A:** direct

---

## Card 49

**Q:** Which component is measured in Ohms to limit the amount of current in an Arduino circuit?

**A:** A resistor.

---

## Card 50

**Q:** What is the mathematical relationship between Voltage ($V$) and Current ($I$) when Resistance ($R$) is constant?

**A:** They are directly proportional.

---

## Card 51

**Q:** The total voltage of a series circuit is shared among components. What is this 'shared' voltage usually called?

**A:** Voltage drop.

---

## Card 52

**Q:** If a circuit has $0A$ of current, how much power is it consuming?

**A:** $0W$

---

## Card 53

**Q:** Which quantity describes the 'volume' of electricity flowing past a point?

**A:** Current.

---

## Card 54

**Q:** Which quantity describes the 'strength' or 'push' of the electricity?

**A:** Voltage.

---

## Card 55

**Q:** If you know the Power ($P$) and the Voltage ($V$), how do you calculate the Current ($I$)?

**A:** $I = \frac{P}{V}$
